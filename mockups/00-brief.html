01 What we found

The current site still carries good material — the club's history, the range rules, the youth program, the memorials. The problems are structural, and one of them is urgent.

The security certificate expired on July 5, 2018

Every visitor who types https://3fclub.org — which is now what every browser tries first — gets a full-page red warning saying the site may be impersonating the club. Most people turn back at that screen.

This is free to fix and takes about fifteen minutes. It should not wait for the rebuild.

Worth saying plainly: the club's information is in good shape. Somebody has been diligently keeping the awards, the memorials, the Summer Classic write-ups and the youth program records current for a decade. The rebuild's job is to stop that work from being buried.

03 Features worth building

These come straight out of what the club already does on paper. Each one replaces a manual process that currently runs through a volunteer's cell phone.

The membership application

Today: a PDF that only appears August 1 through September 30, printed, signed by two current members, and mailed or dropped off. Reviewed in October; acceptance letters by December 1.

  • Keep the window, automate it. The form opens and closes itself on the right dates. Outside the window the page explains when it reopens and offers a reminder email.
  • Sponsors verify digitally. An applicant names two current members; each gets an email and confirms with one click. No chasing signatures. Applicants without sponsors get the letter-of-intent path, exactly as today.
  • An October review queue. The board sees every application on one screen with sponsor status, relation to existing members, and a vote tally.
  • December 1 decisions go out from the system — and declined applicants are told plainly that they may reapply next August.

Range key cards and rules acknowledgment

Today: a notice tells members whose key card stopped working to phone Tim or Liz, sign the current range rules on paper, and wait for reactivation.

  • Rules are versioned. When the range committee publishes a revision, every member's acknowledgment is marked stale automatically.
  • Members re-sign online — read the rules, check the box, done. The card reactivates without a phone call to a volunteer's personal number.
  • Admins see who hasn't signed, and can revoke a card the moment it matters.

Tree stand sign-in

Today: hunters place a physical sticker on a paper aerial map and sign a notebook, so the board and emergency responders know roughly where to search if someone doesn't come back.

  • This is a safety system, and it should work from a phone in the parking lot. Check in, drop a pin on the property map, check out when you leave.
  • An automatic reminder if someone is still signed in well after dark, and a board view showing everyone currently on the property.
  • The paper notebook stays as a backup. Nothing here replaces the sign at the gate.

Everything else that currently lives in a JPG

  • Restaurant menu and hours — real text, updated by whoever runs The Great Foodini, readable on a phone at the bar.
  • Pheasant program — package sizes, release days (Saturday, Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday), and ordering with payment instead of arranging it in person.
  • League scores — winter skeet's 20 weeks, the 10-week summer league, 3-D archery, and the Summer Classic results the club already writes up beautifully every year.
  • The events calendar — with the meeting rule encoded: 7pm the third Tuesday, except November, which is the second Tuesday for hunting season.
  • Pavilion rental — the 40×100 pavilion takes wedding receptions and company picnics. An availability calendar and a request form will book more of them.

04 Three directions to choose from

Same content, same structure, three different personalities. Each is a working page rather than a picture of one — open them on a phone and they hold up.

Direction A

Heritage Crest

Deep hunter green, ivory and aged brass. Engraved serif headings, the club patch treated like a brass plaque. Reads as an institution that has been here a long time.

Best if the board wants dignity and permanence.
Direction B

Field Guide

Light, airy and editorial, built on the contour lines of the property itself. Sage and slate with a single clay-target orange. Feels like a well-made state park guide.

Best for attracting new members and families.
Direction C

Range Status

Dark and operational. Opens with what a member actually needs — are we open, is the range hot, what's on tonight. Safety orange on charcoal, plain utility type.

Best if the site's main job is serving current members.

The member portal and admin console are drawn in Direction A, but the private side is built from the same tokens as the public site — pick any direction and both follow.

05 How I would build it

The deciding factor is that the member and admin features are software, not articles. A calendar plugin can publish an events page; it cannot run an October application review with sponsor verification. Everything below is current-generation and boring in the good way — nothing here needs replacing in three years.

LayerChoiceWhy
LanguageTypeScript (strict)A member record, a dues state, an application status — these are worth having the compiler check. Strict mode from day one, because retrofitting it is miserable.
FrameworkNext.js 15
App Router, React 19, Server Components		Public pages render to static HTML at build time — fast, and search engines finally see real text. The member and admin areas are a real application in the same codebase. Server Actions handle form submissions without a separate API layer.
StylingTailwind CSS v4 + shadcn/uiThe design tokens from whichever direction wins become CSS variables the whole app reads. shadcn gives accessible, unstyled-by-default components we own outright — no dependency that can restyle itself out from under us.
DatabasePostgres on Neon
accessed via Drizzle ORM		Members, dues, applications, sign-ins, orders and scores are relational data. This is the part WordPress was never going to do well. Drizzle keeps the schema in TypeScript and generates real migrations.
Content editingPayload CMS 3Runs natively inside Next.js against the same Postgres database. Gives whoever updates the menu and posts the notices a plain admin screen — no code, no FTP, no theme files.
AccountsAuth.js v5
email links + passkeys		No passwords for members to forget or reuse. A member number links the login to the roster. Role checks gate the admin console at the route level.
PaymentsStripeDues, pheasant packages, apparel, event tickets, shoot entries — money lands in the club account with a record attached. Webhooks reconcile automatically.
EmailResend + React EmailSponsor requests, acceptance letters, closure notices, key card confirmations — templated in the same components as the site.
FilesVercel BlobPhotos, meeting minutes, the bylaws PDF. Member-only documents are served through signed URLs, not guessable paths.
Hosting & CIVercel + GitHub ActionsEvery change gets a preview URL the board can look at before it goes live. Certificates renew themselves, permanently — the 2018 problem cannot recur.
Quality gatesVitest, Playwright, BiomeThe date-window rules and dues logic get unit tests. Sign-in, application, and checkout get end-to-end tests, because those are the flows nobody can afford to have quietly break.
MonitoringSentry + Vercel AnalyticsWhen a member hits an error at 9pm on a Friday, we know before they call someone's cell phone.

On cost: for a club this size every service above has a free or near-free tier except Stripe's per-transaction fee. Expect hosting, database and email to run under $30 a month, against whatever the current cPanel hosting costs.

On lock-in: the content lives in Postgres and the code is a standard Next.js app. If the club ever wants to move hosts, it moves — no proprietary page builder holding the site hostage, which is exactly how the current site ended up frozen in 2015.

07 What I need from the club

Things I can't decide from the outside. None of them block starting.

  • Who owns the domain and the hosting account? Needed to move DNS — and to fix the certificate today.
  • What form is the member roster in? A spreadsheet, a binder, or the treasurer's software. This determines how accounts get created.
  • How are dues collected now, and should they move online? Some clubs deliberately keep this in person.
  • Who will be an administrator? I'd suggest three to five: a webmaster, the membership chair, the range chair, and the treasurer.
  • Are there photographs? There's drone footage of the grounds from 2016. Current photos of the ranges, the pavilion, the ponds and Youth Day would carry the public site enormously.
  • Does the club have a logo file? The 3F patch exists on the site only as a 150-pixel GIF. If there's an original — embroidery art, a decal, a sign — it should be redrawn properly.
