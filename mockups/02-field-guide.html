An index

What's out there

Eight things worth the drive. Each one is run by a committee of members, and most of them are open to your family the day you join.

Skeet & Trap

Five skeet fields and two trap fields, with a 20-week winter league that ends in an awards banquet and a 10-week summer league that mixes both.

Leagues year-round

Outdoor Range

A concrete firing line at 100 yards with a 200-yard line beyond it. Sign in at the gate, red light on, flag up — the rules are posted and enforced by members.

To 200 yards

Indoor Pistol Range

Fifty feet, five stations, mechanical target carriers so nobody walks downrange to change a target. Well lit and properly ventilated.

5 stations

Archery

An indoor range for the off season and a 3-D course through pines and hardwoods, plus stationary targets at 20, 30 and 40 yards.

Indoor & 3-D

The Ponds

Four of them. The largest is a 32-acre lake stocked with largemouth bass and pan fish; all four take waterfowl, dog training and bird watching.

32-acre lake

Pheasant Program

Buy a package of birds; they're released before your hunt. Because the society is non-profit, packages stay affordable. Releases Saturday, Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday.

Optional program

Bird Banding

Purple martins every summer before they leave for Brazil, plus owls and migrants caught in mist nets. Data recorded, band fitted, bird released — and you can stand right there.

Spring & summer

Clubhouse & Pavilion

A bar, a member restaurant, and a 40 by 100 foot pavilion that takes wedding receptions, company picnics and anything else that needs a roof and a crowd.

40 × 100 ft
The club year

Something in every season

Membership is not a summer thing. The calendar runs continuously, and each quarter has its own reason to be here.

  • 3-D archery league schedule posted

  • Applications open August 1

Fall

Sep – Nov

  • Deer season — tree stands signed in and mapped

Winter

Dec – Feb

  • Cross-country skiing across the property
  • Indoor archery and pistol when the weather turns
  • Christmas party and the social calendar
Youth Club

Where the next conservationists come from

The Youth Club meets Mondays and Thursdays. Members teach marksmanship from the ground up — prone first, because it's the steadiest and the hardest to learn properly — along with how to care for a firearm and how to think about what's beyond the target.

Young members qualify for the Winchester/NRA Distinguished Expert award, which means a score of at least 190 out of 200 at fifty feet, ten separate times. Some have earned a New York State lifetime hunting license through the club's own fundraising.

Membership

The door opens once a year

Applications are accepted from August 1 through September 30 — and only then. The board reviews everything in October and notifies accepted members by December 1.

You'll want two current members to sponsor you. If you don't know two yet, send a letter of intent instead, and say so if you're related to a member.
